# LockIn mark explorations

## Concept 1 — Focus latch

A timer arc, focus ring, and clock hand are reduced into one compact gesture: the hand settles into the ring's open terminal, turning “lock in” into an action rather than a padlock. It fits the pomodoro rhythm and the product's calm attention loop without becoming surveillance imagery. At 16 px the almost-closed circle and strong diagonal remain the two dominant signals; the centre becomes a single anchoring pixel cluster.

## Concept 2 — Gaze meets page

A direct gaze line lands on the fold of an open book, joining the two places LockIn recognises as productive attention: screen and study material. The open lower silhouette keeps it friendly and student-specific while the vertical alignment feels measured rather than watchful. At 16 px it reads as a bold book chevron with one centred stem; the small landing dot can disappear without changing the idea.

## Concept 3 — Calibration gate

Two opposing brackets close around a quiet central bar, referencing camera calibration, attention boundaries, and the “In” in LockIn through negative space. The shape feels like a tool state rather than an eye or security badge, making it suitable for a restrained desktop utility. At 16 px the brackets become a sturdy square gate and the central bar stays legible as a two-pixel focus cue.

Concept 1 is the selected direction because its asymmetry is the most memorable, its timer link is immediate, and it survives the widest range of Windows icon sizes.
